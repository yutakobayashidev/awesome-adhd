# Parsing Day-to-Day Dynamics of ADHD and Perceived Stress in College Students: An Ecological Momentary Assessment Study

- PMID: 42695429
- DOI: 10.1177/10870547261484217
- Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42695429/
- Authors: Goh PK; Lee CA; Wong AWWA; Mellor GS; Chung S; Mikami AY
- Journal: Journal of Attention Disorders
- Published: 2026 Sep 4
- Candidate ID: pubmed:42695429

## Abstract

Although ADHD is characterized as a lifelong condition, emerging evidence indicates that symptom severity can fluctuate day-to-day. In this study, the authors examined whether these fluctuations are directionally associated with fluctuations in perceived stress, using 28 days of ecological momentary assessment data. Participants were 187 undergraduate students (mean age 19.7 years; 78% women). Temporal, contemporaneous, and between-subject networks were estimated.

At the sum-score level, ADHD and perceived stress were consistently positively associated across all three network types. In symptom-level analyses, increases in ADHD-related concentration difficulties and procrastination were associated with higher perceived stress on the following day. Conversely, increases in perceived stress, particularly feelings of lacking control, were associated with elevated ADHD symptoms the next day. In the contemporaneous network, procrastination, restlessness, and concentration difficulties showed the strongest associations with perceived stress. Between-person analyses additionally identified dependence on others for organization as a key ADHD-related feature at that level. Negative ADHD–perceived stress relations also appeared in contemporaneous and between-person networks.

The authors conclude that these dynamics may underlie overlap between ADHD and perceived stress during college adjustment, and that key elements could serve as potential early warning signals and micro-level intervention targets.
