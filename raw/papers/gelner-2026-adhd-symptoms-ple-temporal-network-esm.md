# A comparative temporal network analysis of individuals with ADHD symptoms: an examination of the relationship between psychotic-like experiences and related factors using the Experience Sampling Method (ESM)

- Source: PubMed
- PMID: 41677824
- DOI: 10.1007/s00406-026-02203-3
- Authors: Gelner H; Bagrowska P; Misiak B; Samochowiec J; Gawęda Ł
- Published: 2026-06
- Journal: European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience

## Abstract

Psychotic-like experiences (PLEs) refer to hallucination-like or delusion-like experiences defined as subclinical phenomena located at the lower end of the psychosis continuum. PLEs etiology includes neurodevelopmental changes, maladaptive coping styles, exposure to negative emotions, and stress. These factors frequently co-occur in individuals with ADHD symptoms, considered a risk factor for PLEs.

A total of 188 participants from the general population completed the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale questionnaire and participated in a seven-day ESM procedure. Participants were divided into High and Low ADHD groups based on self-report symptom severity scale. Saliva samples were collected to determine cortisol levels. A temporal network analysis was used to analyze targeted associations, and permutation testing compared group differences.

In the high ADHD group, cortisol was a stronger predictor of threat anticipation than in the low ADHD group, and delusion-like experiences were strongly associated with threat anticipation. In the low ADHD group, hallucination-like experiences had a stronger relationship with ruminations and threat anticipation than in the high ADHD group. In both groups, negative affect was the strongest predictor of ruminations. Out-strength analysis suggested that in the high ADHD group, delusion-like experiences had the strongest predictive function on other variables, while in the low ADHD group it was negative affect. Cortisol level was associated with an increase in threat anticipation exclusively in the high ADHD group, and in the low ADHD group with subsequent alcohol consumption.

The authors conclude that the study highlights different temporal dynamics between factors in individuals with high/low ADHD symptomatology in a non-clinical sample. They suggest that interventions for individuals with ADHD should prioritize negative affect as a central element, focusing on adaptive emotion and stress regulation.
