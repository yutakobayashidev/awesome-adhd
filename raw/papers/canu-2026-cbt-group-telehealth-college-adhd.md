# Efficacy of a cognitive-behavioral group intervention for ADHD in college students via telehealth

- Source type: PubMed metadata/abstract
- PubMed ID: 42120372
- DOI: 10.1080/07448481.2026.2657432
- Journal: Journal of American College Health
- Published: 2026
- Authors: Will H. Canu; Kate Flory; Maggie W. Johnson; Laura E. Knouse; Kenneth McClure; Emily A. Miller; Elizabeth A. Bodalski; Abby de Arellano; et al.

## Abstract

College students with ADHD often experience academic and life challenges related to executive functioning (EF). Telehealth interventions are generally beneficial for emerging adults, but this claim needs to be tested for ADHD. The study examined a telehealth adaptation of a cognitive-behavioral intervention for college students with ADHD that focuses on organizational, time management, and planning (OTMP) skills. Students at three universities (n = 106; 72.6% female; mean age = 22.21; 86.7% White; 91.5% Not Hispanic or Latina/e/o) presented for ADHD treatment. Six group sessions and three individual sessions were delivered via video conferencing software. Pre-post differences in self-report measures indicated positive effects, including improvements in ADHD symptoms, impairment, executive functioning, and use of OTMP skills. The authors conclude that a telehealth adaptation of this intervention may be efficacious, but additional studies are needed, including larger and more diverse samples and randomized controlled trials.

## Curation note

Relevant to adult/emerging-adult ADHD, college support, executive function, time management, planning, organization, and telehealth CBT. Evidence should be treated cautiously because the abstract reports pre-post self-report change rather than a large randomized controlled trial.
