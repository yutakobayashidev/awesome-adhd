# Who Puts the “Social” in “Social Computing”?: Using A Neurodiversity Framing to Review Social Computing Research

- arXiv: 2410.15527v1
- DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2410.15527
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15527v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15527v1
- Authors: Philip Baillargeon; Jina Yoon; Amy Zhang
- Venue note: Accepted to CSCW 2025
- Subjects: cs.HC
- Candidate ID: arxiv:2410.15527v1

## Abstract

This paper uses a neurodiversity framing to examine how HCI/CSCW research has perceived people whose ways of thinking, speaking, and being differ from normative assumptions. From a critical analysis of 84 social computing publications over a decade, the authors argue that research with neurodiverse participants is often medicalized, focused on stereotypes of neurodivergent children and families, and insufficiently attentive to the wide spectrum of neurodivergent users.

The authors caution that systems built around restrictive conceptions of disability can prevent users from being social in natural and enjoyable ways. They argue for a critical-disability-informed neurodiversity perspective that treats alternative forms of sociality as meaningful and desirable, and identify opportunities such as collaborating with neurodivergent users and communities, spectrum-conscious social systems, and embedding double empathy into more equitable systems design.
