# Coping with emotional dysregulation among young adults with ADHD

- Source type: PubMed metadata/abstract
- PubMed ID: 37971947
- DOI: 10.1080/09602011.2023.2279181
- Journal: Neuropsychological Rehabilitation
- Published: 2024
- Authors: Maayan Ben-Dor Cohen; Mor Nahum; Ruthie Traub Bar-Ilan; Eran Eldar; Adina Maeir

## Abstract

Emotional dysregulation impacts functional outcomes among individuals with ADHD. This mixed-method study explored emotional-dysregulation-related self-awareness and strategies in daily life among adults with ADHD and examined their interrelations and association with symptoms. Sixty young adults with ADHD participated. At baseline, self-awareness and strategies were assessed with the Self-Regulation Skills Interview (SRSI), and ADHD symptoms with the ASRS checklist. Symptoms were then rated over five days using ecological momentary assessment (EMA). Quantitative and qualitative findings showed significant challenges in self-awareness and strategies. Awareness of emotional dysregulation was associated with variability of ADHD symptoms on EMA but not symptom severity. Qualitative analysis found a range of self-awareness levels related to noticing emotional-dysregulation cues and contextual predictors. Self-awareness and strategies were significantly associated. Strategies varied by effort, individual preference, and temporality. Variability of ADHD symptoms was negatively associated with self-awareness of emotional dysregulation. The authors suggest that strategy selection in daily life may be affected by self-awareness and by a trade-off between short-term effort and long-term effectiveness.

## Curation note

Relevant to emotion regulation, self-awareness, strategy selection, EMA, and daily-life support design. It supports cautious design implications, not personal medical advice.
