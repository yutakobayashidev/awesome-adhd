# Feasibility and Acceptability of Group-Based Cognitive-Behavioral Intervention for Executive Self-Management in College Students With ADHD

- Source type: PubMed metadata/abstract
- PubMed ID: 42312718
- DOI: 10.1177/10870547261457678
- Journal: Journal of Attention Disorders
- Published: 2026
- Authors: Mary V. Solanto; Anthony L. Rostain; Sarah O'Neill; Michael A. Tate; Mariely Hernandez; Zeina Kamareddine; Anna R. C. Workman

## Abstract

This study examined acceptability, feasibility, and replicability of a manualized group cognitive-behavioral intervention designed to enhance executive self-management in college students with ADHD. The open trial was administered at an urban, public college in the northeast United States and also trained PhD-level psychologists to administer the intervention. Forty-one students meeting rigorously assessed DSM-5 criteria for ADHD enrolled in one of six 12-week groups addressing time management, organization, planning, procrastination, distractibility, negative automatic thoughts, reading retention, and organizing/writing essays. Thirty-six students fully or partially completed treatment and provided post-treatment data. Pre- to post-treatment measures showed decreases in clinician-rated and self-rated inattentive symptoms, improvements in executive self-management, BRIEF-A Metacognitive Index, and LASSI-III composite score. Sixty-seven percent rated the program “very helpful” and 30% “moderately helpful.” The authors conclude that feasibility and acceptability were demonstrated, but efficacy/effectiveness cannot be concluded from an uncontrolled trial; a large-scale randomized controlled trial against active control is warranted.
