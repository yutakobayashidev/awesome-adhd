# Effectiveness of a virtual reality-based mindfulness intervention for ADHD symptoms in college students: A randomized controlled trial

- Source type: PubMed-indexed journal article; randomized controlled trial metadata from research-watch candidate.
- PMID: 42591595
- DOI: 10.1177/20552076261478221
- Journal: Digital Health
- Published: 2026
- Authors: YS Lee, J Hwang, BY Youn
- Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42591595/

## Abstract

### Objective

To evaluate whether a virtual reality (VR)-based mindfulness intervention reduces ADHD symptoms and improves cognitive and psychosocial outcomes among college students, compared with a non-immersive, video-based mindfulness condition.

### Methods

Sixty-four college students screening positive for ADHD symptoms on ASRS v1.1 were randomized 1:1 to either a VR mindfulness intervention using the Headspace application on a Meta Quest Pro headset or a comparison condition watching a guided meditation video. After six withdrawals, 58 participants were included in per-protocol analysis, with 29 per group. Both groups completed three supervised sessions per week for four weeks, totaling 12 sessions. Primary outcomes were ADHD symptom severity (ASRS v1.1) and cognitive-motor reaction time measured with BlazePod. Secondary outcomes included depression (PHQ-9), anxiety (GAD-7), sleep quality (PSQI), academic engagement (UWES), and self-efficacy (GSE), measured at baseline, mid-intervention, and post-intervention.

### Results

Compared with the comparison group, the VR intervention group had larger reductions in ASRS scores (-5.8 vs. -2.3 points; p < 0.001). Right-leg reaction time improved more in the VR group (-157.4 vs. -88.4 ms; p = 0.028), but this was not significant after Holm correction (adjusted p = 0.056). The VR group also had significantly larger improvements in depressive symptoms, anxiety, sleep quality, and academic engagement (p < 0.01 for all). Self-efficacy improved in both groups without a significant between-group difference (p = 0.151). Four adverse-event withdrawals were reported: two in the VR group due to severe motion sickness/cybersickness, and two in the comparison group due to severe enteritis unrelated to the intervention.

### Conclusion

A four-week VR-based mindfulness intervention produced greater improvements in ADHD symptoms and related psychosocial outcomes than a video-based comparison among college students. The authors describe the results as supporting further investigation of VR as a digital therapeutic adjunct for adult ADHD symptoms.

## Curation notes

This is a short randomized trial in college students who screened positive for ADHD symptoms, not a broad clinical guideline. The comparison is another mindfulness exposure rather than no treatment, and the per-protocol sample is modest. For wiki use, treat it as evidence that immersive delivery may increase engagement or short-term effects for some students, while noting cybersickness and the need for replication.
