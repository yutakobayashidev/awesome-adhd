# Quantifying the Engagement Trap: Impact of Short-form Video Recommender Systems on Users with ADHD

- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07795v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07795v1
- arXiv ID: 2609.07795v1
- Authors: Vedad Misirlic, Gregor Mayr, Elisabeth Lex
- Published: 2026-09-07
- Categories: Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC), Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)

## Abstract

Short-form video platforms use recommender systems to maximize engagement through highly efficient personalized recommendations. However, the impact of these recommendations on users with ADHD compared to users without ADHD remains underexplored. Through this study, we introduce and operationalize the Engagement Trap, illustrating how recommender systems, while successfully optimizing for engagement, disproportionately disadvantage users with ADHD. This stratified study of 302 participants, recruited via the online platform Prolific, compares experiences between participants with and without ADHD. Our results show that while recommendations are perceived as relevant across groups, participants with ADHD report significantly higher levels of time blindness, post-usage regret, and emotional distress when consuming recommendations. Moreover, we collect feedback for several proof-of-concept, theoretical design interventions for neuro-inclusive design principles. These findings provide quantitative evidence of systemic differences in engagement-optimized recommender systems and highlight the unbalanced negative effects and interactions these systems create for participants with ADHD. We argue for neurodiversity-aware, human-centered design approaches that mitigate such algorithmic harms and support more equitable experiences.

## Notes from ingest

- Study design: stratified quantitative survey of 302 Prolific participants: 150 reported ADHD (65 medically diagnosed, 85 self-reported) and 152 reported no ADHD. Participants were adults aged 18–73 (median 33); medical records and medication status were not collected.
- Questionnaire themes: time blindness/disengagement difficulty, post-usage regret, emotional responses such as guilt/frustration, and preferences for inclusive design features for short-form video platforms.
- Main finding: recommended content was perceived as relevant across groups, but ADHD groups reported greater difficulty stopping, more time blindness, more regret after use, and more negative emotional impact.
- Authors call this pattern the “Engagement Trap”: engagement-optimized personalized streams plus high effort needed for intentional disengagement.
- Proposed inclusive design features were hypothetical, not implemented: focus mode/feed removal, usage reminders, enforced breaks, fixed quantity mode, manual advance/removal of infinite scroll, visual time indicators, and diversity injection/rabbit-hole breakers. Fixed quantity mode and enforced breaks had the highest median ratings among the described options.
- Limits: self-reported survey, no validation of medical diagnosis, no medication-status data, platform use and outcomes were self-reported, and design features were rated from descriptions rather than tested in real use.
