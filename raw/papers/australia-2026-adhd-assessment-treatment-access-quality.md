# The challenge of improving assessment and treatment for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder in Australia

- PMID: 42499140
- DOI: 10.1177/00048674261465180
- Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42499140/
- Journal: The Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry
- Published: 2026
- Candidate ID: pubmed:42499140

## Abstract

Australia's mental health system is under increasing pressure as demand for ADHD assessment and treatment grows. The article notes long wait times, high out-of-pocket costs, and limited specialist capacity. Faster assessments may improve access but can increase misdiagnosis risk and downstream demand.

The policy response discussed is expanding the role of general practitioners to relieve specialist bottlenecks. The article cautions that ADHD assessment requires time-intensive clinical evaluation, including developmental history, collateral information, and differential diagnoses. Adult diagnosis adds complexity because childhood onset often needs retrospective assessment, collateral information may be limited, and life events can affect executive functioning.

The authors argue that access and quality should be pursued together: clearer guideline advice for difficult adult diagnoses, higher rebates for longer assessment appointments, structured training and shared-care support for any expanded GP role, and scalable multimodal ADHD care through multidisciplinary clinics, group programs, workforce upskilling, and national digital psychoeducation and self-management supports.
