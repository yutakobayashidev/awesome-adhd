# Fielded Attention: Reframing ADHD Through a Relational Ontology of Context

## Abstract / source summary

Since DSM-5, ADHD has been classified as a neurodevelopmental disorder, and this placement has often reinforced dominant models that emphasize neurobiological mechanisms, treating attention as an internal trait shaped by executive dysfunction or dopaminergic imbalance. While these frameworks offer valuable insights, they often marginalize the ecological, relational, and sociomaterial contexts in which attention arises or collapses. Context is too often treated as background rather than condition. This paper proposes a field-based reconceptualization of attention: a relational ontology in which patterns of behavior and experience gathered under the ADHD diagnosis are not solely located within the individual but emerge from dynamic tension between organism and environment. Drawing from ecological psychology, dynamic systems theory, and phenomenological psychiatry, it develops the notion of fielded attention, a model in which attention is not a fixed trait but a modulation shaped by rhythm, affordance, and environmental curvature. Translational implications include the design of learning and care environments, including architectural and temporal structure, and adoption of field-sensitive research methods such as ecological momentary assessment and spatial diaries.
